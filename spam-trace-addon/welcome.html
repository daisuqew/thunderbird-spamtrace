Spam Trace

Email header analysis · offline by default

Spam Trace is installed

Spam Trace analyzes the email you are viewing and shows a spam/phishing risk score, sender route, and a map — fully offline by default.

How to use

  1. Open a message, then click the Spam Trace button in the message toolbar to see its risk score, the reasons, and the delivery route map.
  2. Click “Suspicious Email” (which also sets Thunderbird’s Junk flag) or “Legitimate” — Spam Trace learns from your feedback to refine future scores.
  3. High-risk messages can pop up an alert window automatically (you can set the threshold in Settings).

What it checks

The Received route and sender IP/ASN/country, SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication, links in the body (comparing the sender domain with link domains), and brand-impersonation patterns — summarized as a 0–100 score with the reasons and a route map.

About the “Read your email messages” permission

Thunderbird shows this permission for any add-on that can read mail. Spam Trace analyzes the message headers, and reads the body only to check links (for phishing) and to compute a hash. Your message subject and body text are never stored or sent — only hashes are kept, on your computer.

Online features are optional

Online lookups (detailed geolocation, Tor check, statistics upload) are OFF by default. Each asks for its own permission when you turn it on in Options.

IP-to-country uses a bundled DB-IP database (CC BY 4.0); the world map uses Natural Earth (public domain). Both are included in the add-on and used offline. The interface is available in English, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified), Spanish, German, and French.
